Back up your Etsy reviews before closing or restructuring a shop

Back up your reviews before you change anything, because Etsy attaches reviews to listings rather than keeping a portable record at shop level. Once a listing is deactivated, sold out or deleted, the context around its reviews — your seller replies, the original full-resolution buyer photo URLs, and the listing URL itself — is hard or impossible to reconstruct later. The free official download takes a few minutes and is the only step that really has a deadline.

The free download, first

Shop Manager → Settings → Options → Download Data → Take your data with you → Download Your Reviews. Several guides still say Etsy has no review export; that is out of date. Etsy does not publish the column list for this file, so open it in a spreadsheet and write down what you actually got.

The four field groups worth checking

GroupWhy it disappearsWhat to do
Review text and dateLocale date formats get misparsed on importStore dates in ISO 8601 with a timezone
Seller repliesMost sync integrations drop themKeep the raw text verbatim; do not rewrite it
Original buyer photo URLsPreview-size CDN URLs get recompressed or expireSave the full-resolution URL, not the thumbnail
Listing URL / identifierTitles drift; the original URL is the only join keyKeep it even if your review app ignores the column

The last one matters most if you are restructuring a catalog. Say one Etsy listing offered 20 designs and accumulated 300 reviews, and on Shopify you want 20 separate products. The data was never recorded at that granularity, so no tool can cleanly split those reviews afterwards. Preserving the original listing URL at least keeps the mapping you still have.

What a portable archive should contain

At minimum: a stable per-review identifier, review_date, rating, reviewer_name, body, photo_count, picture_urls, verified_buyer, product_id, product_handle, product_title, product_url and seller_reply. That is the 13-column schema described in the CSV columns guide. The identifier is what stops a re-import from duplicating everything.

Store the file outside the browser's Downloads folder, keep it read-only, and note the date you generated it. If you later switch review apps, this is the file you will come back to.

If you already closed the shop

Try the official download first while the account still exists. If the listing pages are gone, do not attempt to rebuild seller replies or original photo URLs from memory or from thumbnails saved on a storefront — those fields are not recoverable after the fact. Work with what you can legitimately access, and be explicit in your records about which fields are incomplete.

The legal bit

An archive is for your own reviews. The FTC's Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, in force since October 2024) treats presenting another seller's customers' feedback as your own as a misrepresentation. Keeping a backup does not change who wrote a review or which product it was about — import per product, keep attribution, and never merge variants that are meaningfully different products.

Frequently asked questions

Should I back up reviews before closing my Etsy shop?

Yes. Reviews are attached to listings, so seller replies, original buyer photo URLs and listing URLs can become unrecoverable once a listing is deactivated or deleted. Use Etsy’s free Download Your Reviews first.

Can I recover seller replies after a listing is deleted?

Usually not. Seller replies are commonly dropped by review-app syncs and are not reconstructible from the storefront. Keep them verbatim in a portable CSV before you change anything.
