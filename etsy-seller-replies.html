How to keep your Etsy seller replies when you export reviews

Check for a seller_reply column first: open Etsy's free "Download Your Reviews" file (Shop Manager → Settings → Options → Download Data → Take your data with you → Download Your Reviews) and see whether your public replies are in it. Etsy does not document the column list. If the replies are there, keep that file verbatim as your source. If they are not, you need another source for that one column — a seller reply cannot be reconstructed from the storefront once a listing is gone.

Why replies get lost

Native review-app syncs are designed to carry ratings and review text, not the shop owner's responses. Judge.me's free Etsy sync, for example, covers written reviews on active listings and does not bring over seller replies. The reply is conversational context: for a negative review it often contains the resolution, a sizing clarification or a shipping explanation. Losing it makes an archived review read very differently from how it read on Etsy.

The same applies to rating-only reviews and reviews on deactivated, sold-out, draft or expired listings. A sync is a projection of a subset of your reviews, not a backup.

Carrying replies in a CSV

Add one dedicated column and do not fold the text into body:

review_date,rating,reviewer_name,body,seller_reply,product_url,product_handle
2026-03-14,5,"@someone","Arrived quickly","Thank you!","https://www.etsy.com/listing/123","silk-scarf-no-7"

Three practical rules:

  • Quote the field. Replies contain commas and line breaks. A naive split on commas will shift every column after it.
  • Keep it verbatim. Do not edit the wording, correct typos or append a marketing line. Editing a seller's own reply changes the record.
  • Do not assume it exists. Most rows will be empty. Empty is correct; a fabricated placeholder is not.

Most review apps have no native field for seller replies. That is fine — keep the column in your archive even if the destination ignores it. The archive is the durable asset; the import is disposable.

Verify before you scale

Run a five-row test import that includes at least one row with a seller reply and one with a comma in the body. Confirm the body is not truncated and the reply survived in the archived file. Re-run the same five rows to check you have a stable per-review identifier and are not duplicating records.

The legal bit

Only archive and import reviews from your own customers, for the products they were written about. 16 CFR Part 465 (effective October 2024) prohibits misrepresenting a review's origin or the product it refers to. Seller replies are your own words and are safe to keep; another shop's reviews are market research, not material for your storefront.

Frequently asked questions

Does Judge.me’s Etsy sync import seller replies?

No. Its native Etsy sync focuses on written reviews from active listings and does not carry over the shop owner’s public replies.

Where do I find seller replies in Etsy’s download?

Open the Download Your Reviews file in a spreadsheet and look for a column containing your public reply text. Etsy does not publish the schema, so the only reliable check is opening the file.
